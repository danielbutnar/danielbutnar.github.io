<Section id="job">

The Contra × Lovable challenge asked entrants to turn “can I book with you?” into “you’re booked”. I invented the client and wrote the brief myself: Rope Street Tattoo, a one-chair studio in Brașov’s old town, where one artist, Mara, does everything.

In the brief she wears gloves six to seven hours a day, so messages wait until night. Every quote needs size, placement, colour and a reference. Sessions run from 45 minutes to 5 hours, so one-hour slots do not work. Cover-ups, hands, necks and faces must never book themselves. About a third of clients are tourists who write in English, German or Romanian and stay two or three days.

</Section>

<Section id="built">

I wrote the brief, the studio’s rules, twelve prompts and a check for each one. Lovable generated the app from them. The tooling that films and voices the demo video is my own code.

- A front door: describe the idea in your own words and language, get a duration, a price range, a deposit and only the times that fit the job.
- A Stripe deposit in test mode. Once paid, the dashed violet “stencil” booking card turns to solid ink.
- A private page per booking to confirm, move, cancel, add to a calendar and sign the consent form, without an account.
- An owner area with Today, Week and a “Needs you” queue, where a pasted DM becomes a priced reply in the customer’s language.
- An autopilot for confirmations, nudges, releases, waitlist refills and aftercare, with a demo clock to fast-forward it.

<Section id="hard">

### The AI reads, the rules decide

A price from a chatbot is a guess. For a tattoo, a wrong guess means an upset customer or a lost day’s pay.

So the AI only reads. Through tool calling it returns fields: language, placement, size, colour, detail, cover-up or not, dates in town, and a confidence score. Deterministic server code turns those fields into numbers, from rules Mara can edit: a size table from 45 minutes to 4 hours, extra time for colour, fine detail and harder placements, rounding up to 15 minutes, 350 lei an hour, and a range from 0.9 to 1.15 times the expected price. Cover-ups, hands, fingers, neck, face, anything over 25 cm, or a confidence below 0.6 go to Mara.

The German test message asks for a 6 cm black line drawing on the ankle. The rules make that 75 minutes plus 15 % for the ankle, rounded up to 90: 450 to 600 lei, with a 150 lei deposit. Editing any field recalculates at once, with no second AI call.

### A week of follow-up in two clicks

Judges cannot wait three days to see a reminder go out.

Every server function asks one clock for the time, and in demo mode that clock is a stored timestamp. One runner handles everything due between its last run and the new time, in order: a confirmation request 72 hours before a session, a second nudge at 48, and at 24 hours an unconfirmed slot is released, the deposit kept, and the time offered to the three best matches on the waitlist. Each action has a dedupe key, and messages are unique per booking and kind, so running the same window twice sends nothing new.

In my tests on the live site, “+1 day” released Ioana’s unconfirmed Wednesday and offered it to Sam, not to Bianca, who only wants Saturdays. Sam paid, and Wednesday was full again.

### Prompts with a test attached

A builder will tell you a feature is done. I needed a way to know.

Each of the 12 prompts ends with a “Done when” line that names exact inputs and results. For the front door, the German message had to give a German summary, 1 h 30 min, 450 to 600 lei, and times only from Tuesday 29 September to Saturday 3 October: the demo clock says Monday, the studio tattoos Tuesday to Saturday, and the sender leaves on Sunday. After each prompt I checked the preview and wrote fix prompts for what failed, such as a demo reset blocked by a foreign key, or a link from a pasted DM that opened an empty step.

### Filming a demo without filming a card

The demo video needed a voice-over that matches the picture, and real test payments, without showing the cardholder name typed on Stripe.

A Python script speaks each narration line with Kokoro, an open-weights voice model, and records its length. A Playwright script then drives the live site and records frames over the Chrome DevTools Protocol. Before each new line, it waits until the previous one has finished, so the voice sets the pace.

```js
// video/film.mjs
async function say(id, vars = {}) {
  if (!(id in SPOKEN)) throw new Error(`no voice line "${id}": run voice.py first`);
  await done();
  if (speakingUntil) await wait(PAUSE * 1000);
  const text = NARRATION[id].replace(/\{(\w+)\}/g, (_, k) => vars[k] ?? `{${k}}`);
  markers.push({ type: "say", id, t: now(), text });
  speakingUntil = now() + SPOKEN[id];
  log(`say ${id} (${SPOKEN[id]} s)`);
}
```

On each Stripe page, recording stops until the payment is done. A build script drops those gaps, shortens page loads and AI waits, and encodes the result with ffmpeg.
